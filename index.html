01
Building trusted executive KPI metrics
+

A number of high-level service KPIs were telling different stories depending on who you asked. Numbers spread across 70+ Power BI dashboards had drifted apart, generating 3-5 leadership escalations a month, and stakeholders had quietly stopped trusting any of them enough to act without double-checking first.

Rebuilding that trust meant more than patching dashboards, so I split the team's effort in two: long-term strategic reporting on one side, self-service for ad-hoc requests on the other, backed by single-source-of-truth views, refresh schedules aligned to the warehouse, and tripwires to catch drift before it reached anyone's desk.

Closing the trust gap itself took just as much work as the platform did: accessible data catalogues with plain-English explanations in a consistent template, dashboards built around a clear narrative rather than a wall of numbers, upfront agreement with stakeholders on how each key measure should be calculated, and data literacy sessions that empowered people to challenge the numbers instead of just accepting them.

Within a short time, KPI consistency climbed 62% → 100%, duplicate and end-of life dashboards fell 70+ → 46, escalations dropped to zero, and workspace health rose 60% → 93%, a result that's held ever since through the same tripwire alerts still watching for variance above 2%.

02
AI-enabled decisioning
+
AI Assistant

In early 2025, Help@Cisco was running lean, fewer support agents after company layoffs, while reskilling toward an AI-enabled support model, and it showed: case spikes were handled reactively, and executive leadership was often the last to know, hearing about problems only once they'd already escalated.

This led me to develop a two-tier tripwire system, standard deviations over six months of case trends, tuned tighter for executives and looser for operations, then led the development of a GPT-based AI Assistant using prompt engineering techniques such as question refinement, cognitive verifier and few-shot examples. We then surfaced on demand in Webex chat and answering questions straight from a verified SQL knowledge base. Case-spike analysis went from half a day to near-instant, and the surprises between operations and executive leadership simply stopped.

Performance-Based Refresh

Cisco's laptop refresh programme had always run on age, not evidence: three years and you're up, regardless of whether a device was struggling or still going strong. That left asset management fragmented, budget handling inefficient, and refresh timing badly out of step with what users actually needed.

Rather than reach for the obvious generative AI answer, I ran a genuine build-versus-buy evaluation, supervised learning, GenAI, pure statistical thresholds, before landing on unsupervised anomaly detection: no training data to maintain, none of GenAI's hallucination risk, and a model that scaled naturally as the device landscape changed. Ranking real performance from CPU, RAM, memory pressure and disk telemetry, combined with usage-based personas, it became the foundation for AI performance scoring, an intelligent refresh decision engine and predictive stock forecasting, turning refresh decisions evidence-based and unlocking millions of dollars in potential savings.

03
Commercial insights that turn into action
+
MacBook Air M5 Standardisation

When RAM costs rose and new supply tariffs pushed Apple's M5 MacBook Pro pricing up, every future Mac refresh using those models was set to get materially more expensive, a cost increase nobody had budgeted for and nobody had proven was actually necessary.

So I had the team quantify CPU and RAM performance across the entire Mac and Windows fleet, benchmarked it against the new M5 MacBook Air, and forecast the cost avoidance of standardising on the Air instead of the Pro. The evidence made the decision straightforward: 1.5x the performance of the M4 generation, double the M3, at 23% less than the M5 14 inch and under half the cost of the 16 inch, comfortably meeting the needs of over 87% of Mac users globally, enough to make the Air Cisco's new standard on the spot.

Finance Budget Proposal

As AI tools became part of daily work, the same signal kept showing up in laptop performance and app-usage data: a growing group of engineers and AI developers whose devices simply couldn't keep pace with what their jobs now demanded.

I used Python to analyse historical device performance, isolated the users with anomalous CPU and RAM growth, and cross-checked those spikes against the applications actually driving them, developer tools and IDEs with AI add-ons among them, before building the case for Finance with Power BI visualisations. I didn't expect it to be approved on the spot, and it wasn't; the goal was to put verified evidence on Finance's radar early and keep advocating with that same evidence into the next financial year, rather than wait for the problem to force a rushed decision.

04
Resilient data foundations
+

After the team migrated to Snowflake, we were constantly on the back foot. Inconsistent changes to data tables triggered rework and re-deployments, and when errors surfaced in the KPIs stakeholders depended on every week, tracing them back through BI tools, transformations and source ingestion took far too long, made worse by external teams wanting direct warehouse access, which put over 200 stakeholders at risk every time credentials rotated.

I tasked the data engineers to rebuild around a DBT transformation and governance layer instead: data lineage for faster root-cause tracing, cataloguing moved from Alation to DBT Docs, and every table change run through an iterative release cycle, development, peer review, automated quality tests, then production, with full version history for troubleshooting. External access was replaced with secure AWS S3 data sharing, removing the disruption risk entirely, and warehouse volume dropped 42% (355 to 205 tables/views), with 517.2M stagnant rows removed and disruptions down from 1-2 a quarter to zero.

05
Building, developing and leading powerful data teams
+

The team's skill profile had grown around the old data ops model where powerful data warehouses and AI-infused tools didn't exist.

Analysts were valued for how fast they could turn around an ad-hoc request, spent too much time dashboard-building, and rewarded for describing a situation in numbers rather than building a case for what to do about it. Speed was the metric. Judgement wasn't.

So I rebuilt the model around a different question: not how fast can we answer this, but how well-placed are we to answer the next ten questions like it. That meant focusing less on building and defining calculations inside the dashboards themselves and far more on developing robust, custom SQL queries and ETL processes underneath them for dashboards and reports to simply consume, deepening the team's advanced SQL and Python skills and data visualisation fundamentals along the way, and building for self-service so people could serve themselves instead of queuing behind an analyst.

A stronger SQL layer changes what a team can build. It doesn't change how they work, so alongside it I coached analysts to build narratives that connect the dots and compel decision-makers to act, not just describe a situation in numbers, took the time to understand what genuinely motivated each person so the business got their best work and they felt real ownership of their craft, and built and evolved a mission statement together with the team and stakeholders rather than handing one down.

What grew out of it was exactly what the old model couldn't produce: a fragmented reporting function turned into a multi-year, AI-enabled analytics capability, recognised as 2x IT Leadership Program Host at Cisco Live (2024-25) and ANZ Mentor of the Quarter (April 2023).

06
Empower everyone to get data to the decision
+
Self-Service Rollout

A third of all data requests were simple enough that no one should have needed an analyst, yet they cost 512 hours of stakeholder wait time and $58K in lost productivity a year, pulling analysts away from the work that actually needed them.

I introduced ThoughtSpot as a self-service layer instead, built entirely on the same verified, governed datasets used for executive KPI dashboards, so speed never came at the cost of trust, then added Spotter as a natural-language GPT layer on top for forecasting and correlation analysis, with new users automatically enrolled in onboarding material from day one. Adoption grew from 46 to 196 users (+326%), monthly queries from 8.1K to 19.2K (+137%), while the data team's own request workload fell 33%, freeing an estimated 738 hours and $68.9K a year for higher-value work.

Direct Vendor Partnership

ThoughtSpot ran as an internal shared service through Cisco's Data & Analytics Office, and as our user base grew across three other departments, that arrangement stopped making sense: we were being cross-charged at a higher rate than dealing with the vendor directly, administration depended on an internal support team with limited capacity that was slow to respond, and the DnA Office wasn't upgrading to the latest ThoughtSpot version, so the newest technical and AI capabilities never reached us.

Rather than keep absorbing that cost and lag, I partnered with other organisational data leads and went direct to ThoughtSpot's account management team to stand up our own instance, negotiating the contract terms and cost ourselves and agreeing to share that cost across the departments now using it, trading a shared service for one we owned and controlled outright.

That direct relationship put full administration and the latest capabilities in our own hands, and gave us the leverage to build the commercial case for a capacity uplift: switching to a $70K/250M-row option delivered 2.86x better value than the $40K/50M-row option, with 65% lower cost per million rows and 5x the capacity for only 1.75x the cost.
